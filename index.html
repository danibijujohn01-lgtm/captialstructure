1. What is Capital Structure?

Capital Structure refers to the permanent long-term financing mix of a company, represented by the proportionate relationship between debt capital, common equity capital, and preference share capital used by a company to finance its operations and investments.

Common Equity

Represents residual ownership. Equity shares carry no fixed obligation to pay dividends, but dilute earnings per share (EPS) and control.

Debt Capital

Fixed-cost borrowing (bonds, debentures, loans). Interest is legally mandatory regardless of profit, but is tax-deductible and avoids equity dilution.

Preference Capital

Hybrid security with a fixed dividend rate payable after corporate tax but before common equity distributions.

2. What is EBITâ€“EPS Analysis?

EBITâ€“EPS Analysis is a vital financial planning technique that investigates how alternative financing options affect a company's Earnings Per Share (EPS) across different levels of Earnings Before Interest and Taxes (EBIT).

Because interest is a fixed financial charge, employing debt causes EPS to fluctuate more widely than EBIT. EBITâ€“EPS analysis charts these relationships to determine which financing plan maximizes shareholder wealth under expected operating performance.

3. What is Break-even EBIT / Indifference Point?

The EBIT Indifference Point is the specific operating profit level where the EPS generated by Plan 1 equals the EPS generated by Plan 2.

The Golden Decision Rule:
  • If Expected EBIT > Indifference EBIT: The financing plan with higher debt / higher financial leverage is superior because the return on capital exceeds debt cost, boosting EPS ("Trading on Equity").
  • If Expected EBIT < Indifference EBIT: The financing plan with lower debt / higher equity is superior, as the burden of fixed interest reduces earnings per share.
  • If Expected EBIT = Indifference EBIT: Both plans produce the identical EPS.
